# Pilot, October 2026

What the first measured run of this design looked like, in numbers, and what it taught. Host: Claude Code only. The Copilot, ChatGPT and local-model adapters have not been measured.

The design under test: three process roles (implementer, reviewer, adversarial-reviewer) plus a mechanical transcriber, explicit models (never inherit), and domain packs chosen per dispatch. The package it grew from had twelve personas and `model: inherit`; the 2026-10-08 comparison is why those were not kept.

## Sample

12 logged tasks on 2026-10-08, one JSON line each (task, role, model, packs, first review, fix rounds, findings, violations, gate exit; the schema is now in [log-schema.md](log-schema.md) and `scripts/summarize_log.py` prints the table from it). Task descriptions in the log are specific to private work, so they are summarised here generically. 8 were implementation or fix tasks, 4 were reviews. 11 ran on the standard tier, 1 on the cheap tier. Every logged gate exited 0.

The pilot's own bar: 10 tasks only justifies continuing; a believable effect needs roughly 30 to 50 diverse tasks. This is not that. Read everything below as direction, not proof.

## Numbers

| Measure | Baseline (before) | Pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-time approval of a reviewed implementation task | 7 of 24 (29%) | 8 of 8 (one of them noted two missing tests) |
| Implementation tasks with no fix round at all | 3 of 24 (13%) | 3 of 8 |
| Fix rounds on implementation tasks | not recorded here | 6 across 8 tasks |
| Rule slips (masked checks, wrong trailer, reviewer edits, missing gate exit code, leaked identifiers) | 12 in 136 work items (9%) | 1 in 12 logged tasks |

The guard that checks dispatches (routing hooks, built outside this repository) recorded 31 dispatches, 0 violations, and 0 dispatches on the most expensive tier. Those 31 come from the guard's decision log, not from the 12-line task log, so the two samples overlap but are not the same.

The one logged slip: a reviewer's mutation sweep restored a source file incompletely and a bad guard line landed in a commit; the next commit fixed it, and the controller verified a clean tree afterwards. That is the origin of the "finish with a clean tree, with proof" rule in the reviewer.

## What held up

- **Implementers were good.** 8 of 8 passed their first review, they reported their own weak spots (one flagged a self-comparison weakness in its own tests; one found a gap before review), and none weakened a check.
- **Reviewers with a shell found things a read-only reviewer cannot.** Three of the four review tasks asked for changes. Running revert checks exposed a vacuous test whose fix path was never reached, found two fail-open exits that an earlier automated pre-scan had missed, and found a type-check gap on the tests that an external adversarial review had missed. That is the reason reviewers keep Bash and the reason the "revert the fix" rule is in the policy.
- **Naming a pack in the brief is not proof it was loaded.** Reports now carry `PACKS LOADED`.

## What did not hold up

- **A same-tier reviewer approves what an adversarial pass then breaks.** Three implementation tasks also got an external adversarial review (Codex). It found something in 3 of 3 after the Sonnet reviewer had approved: 1 high, 8 medium (one ruled correct-by-spec), 5 low in total. The high was a null mutation payload treated as success; the mediums included a followed redirect and a cache check-then-use race. An ordinary reviewer is a gate on the happy path. For security-sensitive diffs, keep an independent adversarial pass, and keep the adversarial-reviewer role as the stand-in for when the external one is out of budget.
- **Restore slips happen.** See above. Mutation checks need a proof of restoration, not a promise.
- **Naming the local-model step in a brief rarely made agents use it** (about 5 of 14 briefs produced a logged call). Verify use from the tool's own log.
- **Small samples hide variance.** The cheap tier ran once, on a one-test fix, and the lead accepted it without a separate re-review. That is one data point, not an endorsement.

## What is not measured

Copilot custom agents, ChatGPT and local models running these prompts; any pack other than by name in a brief; the effect of the policies embedded in every agent, as opposed to the earlier standalone rules; cost. The exercises in [behavioral-evals.md](behavioral-evals.md) are the way to start measuring them.
